Slavic collections: DK, PG and Ukraine (DK508)

The Jefferson comparison explains little of how the catalog grew. This page looks at another explanation, the history of what the Library acquired, using the Slavic classes. Source: Library of Congress MARC distribution, 2016.

1. Where the Slavic classes rank

Rank among all 238 subclasses by number of records (1 = most). PG, the Slavic languages and literatures, climbs from about 29th at the creation of the classification in 1897 to 13th. DK, Russia and the former Soviet states, moves more slowly, from 46th to 30th.

DKPG

2. Records per publication year

Records published in each year, 1850–2016. Both classes grow slowly until the 1960s, then steeply, with a second step after 1991. There is no visible jump at 1906. The grey band marks the years after 2011, which the 2016 file covers incompletely.

DKPG

The spike at 1900 appears in DK, PG and other history classes alike. It looks like a dating artifact (an uncertain date coded as 1900), not an event, but I have not verified that against the records.

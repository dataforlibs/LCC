History before literature, or literature before history? The whole world

For 52 countries and regions, the year when the Library of Congress history class and the literature class for the same country each reached half of their records. Source: Library of Congress MARC distribution, 2016.

1. The gap on a world map

Orange countries are those where the history class reached its midpoint first, blue where the literature class did; the colour deepens with the size of the gap (up to 14 years). The number is the gap in years. Pale grey countries have no pair yet, mostly because their literature is classed by language in a range not counted here (see section 5).

Languages cross borders, so some colours stand for a language area rather than one state: the Arab world (one Arabic literature class against eleven history classes added together), sub-Saharan Africa (African-language literature against eighteen history classes), Central America, and the Cuba, Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico group. Small countries and the European countries other than Russia are coloured but not labelled; hover a coloured country to see its curves, or click to keep the popup open.

2. The midpoints, pair by pair

Each cumulative curve (records published through 2011) is divided by its total and fitted with a logistic curve. The inflection year t0 is the year when about half of the records had been published. Pairs are sorted by the gap, history first at the top. A dagger marks pairs whose literature class is defined by language, so it can include writing from other countries. The dashed line is the median midpoint of all other LC subclasses ().

What the chart shows
  • English-speaking countries: history first. Canada leads the whole table at 14.9 years (English literature alone 15.8, French literature alone 13.1). Australia (12.0), New Zealand (11.9) and the United States (11.7) join England (9.7) and Scotland (10.5) at the history-first end.
  • Latin America: split, with literature first in half of the pairs. Ecuador (11.9), Bolivia (9.1), Uruguay (7.5), Brazil (6.2), Argentina (4.8), Colombia (3.8) and Venezuela (2.8) have literature ahead. Chile, Peru, Central America and Mexico are within 2.5 years. The Caribbean group and Paraguay (a literature class of only 780 records) have history ahead.
  • South Asia: literature first. Hindi literature is 4.9 years ahead of the history of India, Urdu 9.5 years ahead of Pakistan, Bengali 16.2 years ahead of Bangladesh. The Bengali and Urdu classes also hold writing from India, so these gaps measure a language more than a country.
  • East Asia and the Middle East: close. China (2.7) and Korea (3.6) lean to literature. Japan, Israel, Iran and the Arab world are within 2.5 years.
  • Sub-Saharan Africa: literature first by 5.8 years, with Ethiopia the other way (history 4.3 years ahead, on a small literature class of 727 records).
  • Turkey and the Balkans: close to even. Literature leads in Turkey by 5.2 years and in Romania by 2.5, and history leads in Bulgaria by 1.2. The Romanian and Bulgarian classes include the language as well as its literature.
  • Europe: history first in the west, literature first in the east (details on the Europe page).

5. What this does not show

  • The curves are by publication year of the records held, not by the year they were acquired or cataloged, and fits stop at 2011 because the distribution is incomplete after about then.
  • Literature classes for Asia and Africa are mostly defined by language (PJ, PK, PL), and many languages are spoken in several states. Hindi covers one language of India, Urdu is shared by Pakistan and India, Bengali by Bangladesh and West Bengal, Persian by Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan. English-language writing of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, South Africa, Canada, Australia and New Zealand is classed in PR9000–9899, which is counted here only for those three.
  • No literature pair yet for Southeast Asia and Sri Lanka. Australian literature is PR9600–9619 and New Zealand PR9620–9639, which hold only the national-literature classes; Australian and New Zealand history is the DU class alone. Midpoints of their history classes alone are in the table below.
  • Canadian literature is classed by language, so English (PR9180–9199) and French (PQ3900–3919) are added together. The Canadian PS8000 schedule is a local Canadian alternative that the Library of Congress does not use.
  • Histories of the Arab world and sub-Saharan Africa are sums of classes for individual states, which makes their curves smoother than any single country.
  • Spanish and Portuguese literature classes for Spain and Portugal include Latin America and Brazil, and German includes Austria and Switzerland (Europe page).
